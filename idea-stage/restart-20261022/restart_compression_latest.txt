L9: # Title:PLEDGE: Progressive Lossy Error-Bounded Compression of 3D Gaussian Splatting
L10: 
L11: Authors:【5†Mingze Xia】, 【6†Yuxiao Li】, 【7†Baixi Sun】, 【8†Wenbo Li】, 【9†Pu Jiao】, 【10†Sheng Di】, 【11†Franck Cappello】, 【12†Hanqi Guo】, 【13†Xin Liang】

L13: View a PDF of the paper titled PLEDGE: Progressive Lossy Error-Bounded Compression of 3D Gaussian Splatting, by Mingze Xia and 8 other authors
L14: 
L15: 【14†View PDF】 【15†HTML (experimental)】
L16: > Abstract:Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) codecs report reconstruction quality after compression, and give users little control over the distortion of individual primitives. We present PLEDGE, a training-free, progressive, error-bounded codec that translates four user-declared tolerances into per-primitive quantization bounds: screen-space position, footprint deformation due to rotation and scale, view-dependent color, and opacity.
L17: The bounds apply throughout a declared camera domain, including previously unseen viewpoints. Position and opacity are bounded directly; footprint deformation is bounded under the rasterizer's affine projection model, and color is bounded over all viewing directions. PLEDGE organizes quantized residuals into a progressive bitstream with explicit error bounds for every valid prefix.
L18: Experiments across thirteen scenes verify all declared per-mechanism bounds and demonstrate competitive rate-distortion performance. The four tolerances incur substantially different rate costs, so users can spend rate where their application needs it.

L9: # Title:SplatQ: Interaction-Aware Pruning of 3D Gaussian Splatting via Quadratic Selection
L10: 
L11: Authors:【5†Hongbin Ye】, 【6†Haixu Liu】, 【7†Shenwei Kang】, 【8†Wenxiu Wei】, 【9†Tinglan Wang】, 【10†Hongdong Zhu】, 【11†Fan Tang】, 【12†Qi Gao】, 【13†Yin Ma】, 【14†Kai Wen】, 【15†Hai Wei】, 【16†Nuo Qian】, 【17†Wei-Shi Zheng】, 【18†Shaobo Chen】, 【19†Fa-Ting Hong】, 【20†Jingke Meng】
L12: 
L13: View a PDF of the paper titled SplatQ: Interaction-Aware Pruning of 3D Gaussian Splatting via Quadratic Selection, by Hongbin Ye and 15 other authors
L14: 【21†View PDF】 【22†HTML (experimental)】
L15: > Abstract:Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) pruning methods primarily rely on global Top-K selection based on independent importance scores, lacking regional budget control and ignoring joint deletion effects. Consequently, they cannot ensure balanced reconstruction quality across a scene. We propose SplatQ, a combinatorial pruning algorithm that coordinates regional budget allocation and joint Gaussian selection under a fixed budget.
L16: SplatQ partitions Gaussians by spatial proximity and appearance similarity into size-limited blocks, decomposing global selection into subproblems of a specific size. It then smooths importance-based block retention quotas to prevent excessive budget concentration. Finally, it constructs a quadratic approximation of joint deletion effects using the responses of representative pixel colors to changes in Gaussian opacity.
L17: Individual responses, pairwise interactions, and quota penalties are incorporated into a quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) objective, solved using simulated annealing (SA) or a coherent Ising machine (CIM) to jointly select Gaussians. Experiments on 5 benchmarks demonstrate that SplatQ outperforms state-of-the-art pruning methods in aggregate reconstruction quality, spatial quality balance, and rendering speed.
L18: Ablations confirm that budget smoothing improves local detail reconstruction, while interaction modeling improves Gaussian selection quality under a fixed budget.

L9: # Title:Compact Neural Appearance Models for Efficient Gaussian Splatting
L10: 
L11: Authors:【5†Florian Hahlbohm】, 【6†Jorge Condor】, 【7†Linus Franke】, 【8†Martin Eisemann】, 【9†Marcus Magnor】
L12: 
L13: View a PDF of the paper titled Compact Neural Appearance Models for Efficient Gaussian Splatting, by Florian Hahlbohm and 4 other authors
L14: 
L15: 【10†View PDF】 【11†HTML (experimental)】
L16: > Abstract:Explicit primitive-based radiance fields such as 3D Gaussian Splatting typically model view-dependent appearance using low-order spherical harmonics (SH). Although efficient to evaluate, SH coefficients dominate per-primitive storage and memory traffic, while their band-limited basis restricts angular detail.
L17: We present a thorough, end-to-end comparison of SH and recent spherical appearance models and introduce an implicit alternative that decodes compact per-primitive latent codes using a tiny shared MLP. We integrate all models into the same optimized pipeline, fusing their forward and backward passes into a differentiable CUDA rasterizer and provide a portable WebGL viewer for laptop and mobile GPUs.
L18: Our evaluation across reconstruction quality, memory use, and optimization and rendering performance shows that recent spherical models offer the strongest overall quality-efficiency trade-off. Our neural representation is the most compact model evaluated and, compared to third-degree SH, reduces the per-primitive appearance footprint from 192 to 28 bytes, accelerates optimization by 1.3$\times$, while improving reconstruction quality.
L19: We further analyze how appearance parametrization shapes optimization, identifying differences in recovered geometry and the tendency of expressive models to absorb non-static scene content. Together, our framework and analysis provide practical guidance for replacing SH beyond what image metrics alone can capture.
